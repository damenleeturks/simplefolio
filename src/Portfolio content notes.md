# ADOCS
## Description: 
A controlled document library. With this project we started from scratch, laying the foundation for complex versioning system that needed to integrate with a training management system as well as several disparate workflow and event tracking apps.

## Challenges:
The current solution had become bogged down with non-standard uses. We wanted to focus the product on being a document library, not a file storage system. 

Because clients' employees would be training on specific versions of documents, this meant we had to get versioning right from the beginning. We spent a lot of time interviewing stakeholders and current clients to identify their needs, and eventually came up with a system that allowed access to previous versions while still promoting the current version prominently. 

This truly was a cross-discipline effort, involving not only design and product management, but also front-end development and architecture. 

# Lists
Shared and linked data was key here. These were the inital steps in a centralized data management system. Our clients use the same options and ask the same multiple choice questions in hundreds of forms across hundreds of processes and workflows. To keep all that aligned and keep the data consistent, we needed a way to ensure those options were the same every time they were shown *and* would be updated everywhere when needed. This helped with both tracking and analytics and sped up implementation and updates across the board. 

To do this, we had to create a simple managment application and custom data capture configuration for our form builder. 

Knowing that this would not be the end of the effort, we also devised a multi-stage plan that took us from simple lists of strings up to lists of complex data objects with multiple points of data for each item. 


# Cross-Platform Design System
Consistency in the details builds trust with your constomers, and any large buisiness with multiple applications tends to struggle with this. While the company had employed other design system managers before, the effort had not always been well received. 

  - too authoritarian or too disconnected (ai docs without review)
  - No documentation (let the components speak for themselves does nothing for consistent usage)
  - No integration with dev

When I took the position, I decided to treat my coworkers as my clients. And I 
  - philosophy of stocking a pantry, not controlling a dieting regimen or setting a meal plan
  - Regular working meetings with teams ensured check-ins were informal and more informative. 
  - Open for exploration by individuals when the existing components didn't meet their needs. This allowed for speed on the product teams. 
  - Lots and lots of consulting, I touched almost everything (PDF Publishing, Catalog integration) and helped everyone. 

By choosing not to directly govern and police the design system's usage, that left time for me to refine the system itself.  This meant enhancing the components what we have in Figma and connecting more with the developers on the design system team to implement those components accurately. As time progressed, this also came to mean I was in the code base itself filling design gaps and erasing design debty by fixing the small details that never woud have surfaced in a JIRA priority list.
